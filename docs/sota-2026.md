# State of the art, October 2026, and where this project fits

**Method and its limits.** This came from web search excerpts and from reading two
public repositories in full: vLLM main at `34051ad`, and `lvlrSajjad/hazardserve`.
Paper pages on arxiv.org, alphaxiv and mlsys.org were blocked from the research
environment, so the paper claims below come from abstracts and excerpts. **Read
ShuntServe, KevlarFlow, GhostServe, LUMEN and hazardserve in full before freezing
any novelty claim.**

## 1. What changed since the original plan (SpotServe/Llumnix era)

The original plan's gap was "predictive and correlated spot preemption, exploited by
request-level KV-aware migration". Most of it is now occupied:

| Work (date) | What it does | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| **ShuntServe** (arXiv 2606.18600, Jun 2026; code at ddps-lab/ShuntServe) | LLM serving on *heterogeneous spot* GPUs (AWS g6/g6e). Output-preserving request migration plus concurrent initialization via a shared tensor store. About 31% better cost efficiency than on-demand. | Measured KV transfer against recompute for migration: **recompute wins in most cases**. Transfer wins only with very large models, very long contexts, weak GPUs or fast networks. A per-request hybrid choice is left as future work. This directly undercuts "KV-aware drain beats recompute" as a general claim. |
| **hazardserve** (GitHub, Sep 2026, v0.1 simulator) | Per-node survival hazard (Kaplan-Meier conditioned on uptime), one expected-cost function for placement *and* pre-emptive migration, and a choice between transfer and recompute. | **This is the original "predictive" idea, already published as a scaffold.** Its own Phase 0 finding is that pre-emptive migration "barely fires": a planned move costs about one re-prefill, which exceeds the risk it removes, except with a fast link, a long request and a node late in its life. Synthetic traces only. |
| **KevlarFlow** (arXiv 2601.22438, Jan 2026) | Background KV replication to peers, plus decoupled model parallelism and rerouting. MTTR down 20×; under failures, p99 TTFT about 575× better. | Continuous replication handles *sudden* failures and pays the cost all the time. |
| **GhostServe** (MLSys'26, arXiv 2605.00831) | Checkpoints KV with erasure coding (parity in host DRAM) plus partial recompute. Under 6% overhead. | Another continuous-protection design. |
| **LUMEN** (arXiv 2606.17787, Jun 2026) | Load-aware recovery: where to place checkpoints, how to spread interrupted requests, how to restore capacity. | Recovery *coordination* is taken. |
| **ConServe** (ICML'26) | Incremental, per-token KV checkpointing to host memory for preemptible offline jobs. | Shows that append-only KV logging is cheap. |
| **Dynamo fast recovery** (arXiv 2609.25451, Sep 2026, NVIDIA) | Over 18 weeks of production failures, most were *device-preserving*. A GPU memory service reattaches surviving allocations: recovery in under 7 s. | Doesn't apply to spot, where the device itself disappears. Useful contrast. |
| **SkyNomad** (arXiv 2601.06520) | Spot lifetimes are heavy-tailed and vary by region and time. Uses lifetime prediction for **batch** jobs. | Characterization of lifetimes is largely done. |
| **SemSpot** (arXiv 2608.29581, Aug 2026) | A spot "offer" model at the API level for **agent** workloads (price, completion probability, failure-notice deadline). | Shows agents on spot capacity is an emerging theme. It works at the API layer, not the KV layer. |
| vLLM upstream (`34051ad`) | Offload-on-preempt (`OffloadingConnector.handle_preemptions`), a **P2P KV tier over NIXL** (`v1/kv_offload/tiering/p2p`), `pause_scheduler(mode="wait")`, and graceful-drain RFCs (#24885, router #252). | The KV transport between instances exists; "drain locally" exists. Nothing reacts to a termination deadline. |

Wider 2026 context: prefill/decode disaggregation is standard (vLLM, SGLang, Dynamo,
llm-d, with NIXL as the transport). **Agentic, multi-turn workloads dominate cost**,
and the *prefix-cache hit rate* is now the main cost lever: vLLM added session-aware
prefix retention (AgentX blog, Sep 2026). A public trace of 68k requests from 393
Claude Code sessions exists (`gauravapiscean/agentic-kv-cache`).

## 2. What is still open (best evidence available, not yet verified)

All of the prior work treats the **in-flight request** as the thing at risk, and
either (a) protects it continuously (KevlarFlow, GhostServe, DéjàVu, ConServe), or
(b) reacts after the loss (SpotServe, ShuntServe, LUMEN), or (c) tries to predict
the loss (hazardserve). Two things seem to be missing:

1. **The asset at risk has changed.** In agentic serving, most of the value on a
   node is *idle session prefix caches*: tens to hundreds of thousands of tokens per
   session, waiting for the next turn. Losing them doesn't fail any request. It
   silently turns every later turn into a full re-prefill. None of the spot or
   fault-tolerance systems above measure or protect this.
2. **The termination notice is free time that nobody has budgeted.** On AWS a spot
   reclaim usually arrives with a rebalance recommendation and a 2-minute notice.
   Hazardserve shows that *speculative* migration rarely pays. Continuous
   replication pays its cost all the time. Neither asks: *given W seconds of
   notice and bandwidth B, what should be saved?* That is a deadline-bounded
   knapsack over KV objects. Each object (an in-flight request's KV, or an idle
   session prefix) has a size, a recompute cost, an expected reuse value, and safe
   destinations. Peers in the same reclaim wave are not safe destinations, which is
   where correlated preemption enters.

## 3. Reframed research question

> **With a termination notice of W seconds, how much of the KV value at risk on a
> spot node (in-flight requests plus idle agent-session caches) can be preserved by
> deadline-bounded evacuation, and in which regimes does on-notice evacuation match
> continuous replication, at zero steady-state cost?**

Why this is a good research question:

- **It turns the competitors into baselines rather than threats.** ShuntServe and
  recompute are the floor. KevlarFlow- or ConServe-style continuous replication is
  the ceiling, at a steady-state cost. Hazardserve's negative result is the
  motivation.
- **It produces a regime map, not a claim to beat everyone.** The axes are notice
  W (0, 30 s, 120 s), bandwidth, model KV bytes per token, the distribution of
  session sizes, and burst size under correlated reclaim. The 0 s column is where
  continuous replication *must* win. That honest boundary is a feature.
- **It can be measured on modest hardware**, using single-GPU instances, real KV
  transport (LMCache or NIXL over TCP), real agent traces, and spot behaviour
  replayed on on-demand instances (established practice).
- **The result survives a null.** If evacuation never matches replication, the
  characterization of session-KV value at risk under spot reclaim still stands.

## 4. Claims we must **not** make
- "First to predict preemption" (Parcae, SkyNomad, hazardserve).
- "First to exploit correlation" (SkyServe).
- "KV transfer beats recompute" in general (ShuntServe measured the opposite for most
  regimes; we must reproduce their crossover first).
- "First fault-tolerant KV" (DéjàVu, KevlarFlow, GhostServe, ConServe).
